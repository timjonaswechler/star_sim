# Non-overlapping planet-occurrence source strategy

## Question

Which primary transit, radial-velocity (RV), microlensing, and direct-imaging occurrence models should own each host × observable × orbit domain so that the simulator can realise terrestrial, Neptune-like, giant, and cold planets without counting the same population more than once?

## Recommended decision

Adopt an **exclusive occurrence-owner registry**. Exactly one source model owns any calibrated `host predicate × source-native planet observable × orbit interval`. Other studies may validate or condition that owner, but they must not independently emit planets in the same domain. The registry must use the source's actual host-selection predicate and native observable (`radius`, `M sin i`, true mass, or mass ratio), rather than first converting every paper into informal planet classes.

Use the following first owner set:

1. [Hsu et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ab31ab) owns radius–period occurrence for its clean FGK-main-sequence selection over `0.5–16 R_earth` and `0.5–500 d`.
2. [Dressing & Charbonneau (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/807/1/45) owns radius–period occurrence for its early-M-dwarf selection over the reported `0.5–4 R_earth`, `0.5–200 d` cells; cells with only an upper limit or no point estimate remain censored/unsupported rather than becoming zero-rate cells.
3. [Bryant et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00659) owns close-in low-mass-host giants over `0.6–2.0 R_J`, `1–10 d`, and `0.088–0.71 M_sun`.
4. [Clanton & Gaudi (2016)](https://doi.org/10.3847/0004-637X/819/2/125) owns the first wide-orbit M-dwarf mass–semimajor-axis model over `1 M_earth–13 M_J` and `a >= 1 AU`, subject to its inferred outer cutoff and single-M-dwarf applicability. It is already a joint microlensing + RV + imaging synthesis, so its constituent surveys are not added again.
5. [Fernandes et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ab0300) owns FGK giants beyond the transit grid, using `0.1–20 M_J` and `500 < P <= 10,000 d`. Its extension beyond the RV baseline and out to `100 AU` is a `PhysicalProxy`, not an empirical continuation.
6. [Cassan et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10684) may supply a clearly labelled `PhysicalProxy` for cold `5–30 M_earth` planets around otherwise uncovered main-sequence hosts, using its differential mass function over `0.5–10 AU`; it is not an empirical per-FGK-host calibration because its lens population is Galactic and host-marginalised.
7. [Nielsen et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ab16e9) owns the currently separate wide, massive-planet domain around young high-mass stars: `M_star > 1.5 M_sun`, `5–13 M_J`, and `10–100 AU`.

Everything outside an owner's exact host and planet domain is typed `UnsupportedOccurrence` unless it is generated by a separately named `PhysicalProxy` or `Speculative` model. In particular, evolved stars, stellar remnants, close binaries, and most short-period A/B-star planets do not inherit single-dwarf rates silently.

## Why ownership must be expressed in native observables

Kepler transit occurrence is measured in planet radius and period. Hsu et al. infer rates in `0.5–16 R_earth` and `0.5–500 d` cells for a clean FGK main-sequence sample, including the Kepler pipeline and robovetter selection functions; their long-period `0.75–1.5 R_earth`, `237–500 d` result is an upper limit rather than a precise Earth-analogue rate. ([Hsu et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ab31ab))

RV occurrence is measured in minimum mass and period, while microlensing directly constrains planet/star mass ratio and projected separation in Einstein-radius units. Suzuki et al. explicitly fit `dN/(d log q d log s)` and found a break near `q ~ 10^-4`; converting that result into a radius–period grid requires additional host-mass, Galactic-geometry, orbital-projection, and mass–radius assumptions. ([Suzuki et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/833/2/145))

Therefore, a boundary such as “sub-Neptune versus giant” cannot by itself prevent overlap between a radius survey and a mass survey. A `4 R_earth` body is not assigned a unique mass by either occurrence study. The non-overlap key must retain the native observable and a later mass–radius/composition model must reconcile candidate identities. This is an inference from the incompatible measured coordinates in the cited transit and microlensing analyses, not a boundary published by either paper. ([Hsu et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ab31ab); [Suzuki et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/833/2/145))

### Deliberate gaps

The matrix intentionally leaves gaps. Dressing & Charbonneau report no revised-radius point estimate for sub-Earths in their two longest period cells, so those cells are censored rather than assigned zero occurrence. ([Dressing & Charbonneau 2015](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/807/1/45))

For FGK stars, Hsu reaches Earth-like radii and approximately year-long periods, so an Earth–Sun-like outcome is possible but not forced; the `0.75–1.5 R_earth`, `237–500 d` cell is weakly constrained and reported as `<0.27` planets per FGK star at the 84.13th percentile. ([Hsu et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ab31ab))

Cold low-mass planets around specific FGK hosts beyond Hsu's limit are not directly calibrated by the selected transit source. Cassan makes such bodies possible only as a `PhysicalProxy`; calling them `Empirical` for an individual local FGK host would overstate what the microlensing analysis measured. ([Cassan et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10684))

## Rules that prevent double counting

1. **One generator per ownership cell.** `occurrence_source_id` is mandatory on every `Explicit Planet Candidate`; two active owners with intersecting host, observable, and orbit predicates are a configuration error.
2. **Source replacement, not stacking.** Installing Hsu disables the current Petigura/CKS FGK emitters in their overlap. Installing the Dressing cell grid disables its `1–4 R_earth`, `P < 200 d` aggregate count as a separate emitter. The older results remain ensemble-validation targets. ([Hsu et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ab31ab); [Dressing & Charbonneau 2015](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/807/1/45))
3. **Use published syntheses as atomic owners.** Clanton & Gaudi already combine microlensing, RV, and imaging likelihoods; adding the constituent survey rates as further candidates would count the same constraint twice. ([Clanton & Gaudi 2016](https://doi.org/10.3847/0004-637X/819/2/125))
4. **Condition distributions analytically.** When a source model spans an owned and an unowned interval, integrate/renormalise its differential model over the owned interval. Do not draw from the whole source and reject planets in another owner's domain, because that changes the intended mean and makes rejected occurrence dependent on implementation order.
5. **Upper limits are distributions, not zeros or medians.** Preserve posterior samples or a censored representation for Hsu/Dressing low-completeness cells; do not treat an upper limit as a Poisson mean. ([Hsu et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ab31ab); [Dressing & Charbonneau 2015](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/807/1/45))
6. **Separate occurrence from architecture rejection.** A drawn empirical candidate keeps its source and rate provenance if a later whole-system stability check rejects it. A stability rejection must not trigger a replacement draw from another occurrence source.

## Roles for sources that are not owners

The existing Johnson et al. metallicity/mass relation should stop independently emitting giant planets once the Fernandes and Clanton owners are active. Johnson measured a host fraction for Doppler-detectable giants (`K > 20 m/s`, roughly `a <= 2.5 AU`) as a function of stellar mass and `[Fe/H]`; it can validate or motivate a later host-property reweighting model, but multiplying it into another source's occurrence rate is a new composite proxy and requires marginal renormalisation. ([Johnson et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1086/655775))

Suzuki et al. should initially validate the cold mass-ratio shape, especially the break near Neptune mass ratio, rather than emit an additional microlensing population on top of Clanton/Cassan. A future replacement may refit the source registry with Suzuki's newer `q,s` likelihood, but that is a model-version change. ([Suzuki et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/833/2/145))

Direct-imaging surveys should validate the wide tail for FGKM hosts where Fernandes or Clanton already owns the population. Fernandes found its broken-period extrapolation consistent with the low occurrence measured by imaging, but periods beyond the RV baseline remain extrapolation; agreement is not equivalent to a direct empirical measurement for every host. ([Fernandes et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ab0300))

## Required registry shape

The decision implies a configuration-level structure similar to:

```text
OccurrenceOwner {
    model_id,
    model_version,
    host_predicate,          // source selection, not just "FGK" or "M"
    native_observable,       // Radius | MinimumMass | TrueMass | MassRatio
    native_observable_range,
    orbit_coordinate,        // Period | SemimajorAxis | ProjectedEinsteinSeparation
    orbit_range,
    evidence_level,          // Empirical | PhysicalProxy | Speculative
    posterior_representation,
    source_citation,
    overlap_priority: Exclusive,
}
```

At startup, validate pairwise intersections after host predicates have been normalised. Runtime generation first resolves the single owner, then samples that owner's occurrence posterior and source-native planet coordinates. Cross-observable properties are added later and carry their own evidence provenance.

## Decisions this research does not make

- It does not choose the mass–radius/composition model that reconciles radius-owned and mass-owned candidates.
- It does not decide how system-level planet multiplicity and correlated architectures replace independent cell counts.
- It does not define a metallicity/stellar-mass reweighting that preserves each source's marginal occurrence.
- It does not extend single-star occurrence to binary or circumbinary architectures.
- It does not define birth inventories or survival transformations for evolved stars and stellar remnants.

Those are separate decisions because each can change an occurrence owner's effective intensity or candidate identity and thereby reintroduce double counting if hidden inside sampling code.

## Sharp follow-up decisions surfaced

1. **Choose a probabilistic mass–radius–composition bridge and identity rule.** Decide when a radius-owned and mass-owned description can refer to the same latent body, and how an inferred property receives `PhysicalProxy` provenance.
2. **Choose host-property reweighting with marginal preservation.** Decide whether and how `[Fe/H]`, stellar mass, age, and Galactic population reweight an owner's cells without adding Johnson-like occurrence a second time.
3. **Choose posterior and censoring semantics.** Decide whether one occurrence-rate posterior is sampled per ensemble, per stellar system, or represented as epistemic uncertainty outside the seed, and how upper-limit-only cells generate (or do not generate) candidates.
4. **Choose binary-host occurrence ownership.** Decide which empirical/proxy models govern S-type and P-type systems as a function of stellar separation, instead of extrapolating single-star owners.

## Fog remaining

- Whether a newer self-consistent microlensing reanalysis should replace the Cassan/Clanton baseline after the first registry version is validated.
- Whether the final destination needs empirical occurrence for short-period planets around A/B stars, evolved stars, and remnant hosts, or whether typed proxy/unsupported coverage is sufficient for those rare branches.
- How far the simulator should materialise ultra-wide bound planets before Galactic tides and stellar encounters belong to the whole-system plausibility model.
