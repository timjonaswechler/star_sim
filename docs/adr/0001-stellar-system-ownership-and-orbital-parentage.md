# Separate stellar-system ownership from orbital parentage

A `Stellar System` is the sole aggregate owner of every generated object, while present-day orbital parentage, host scope, population membership, resonance association, and claim derivation are independent relationships. This separation lets circumstellar and circumbinary structures coexist without making barycentres or statistical populations into object owners or physical central bodies.

## Consequences

- A physical `Orbital Node` has exactly one present-day `Orbital Parent`: another physical body or any nested barycentre. Every accepted node and collective orbital structure resolves within the same Stellar System; parent graphs are acyclic.
- A planet or dwarf planet orbits a stellar member or barycentre. A Natural Satellite orbits a planet or dwarf planet. A Ring System is a collective domain associated with that same central body, not an Orbital Node.
- A Trojan Population shares its planet's stellar Orbital Parent and separately references the planet as its 1:1 resonance partner.
- A Planetesimal Reservoir is a collective domain with a stellar Host Scope. A materialised member orbits that stellar member or barycentre and separately references its reservoir. A Debris Disk references one or more compatible source reservoirs and shares their Host Scope.
- Ordinary ring particles and unmaterialised population members remain statistical data. Every individually materialised body is an Orbital Node; a notable ring moonlet is represented as a Natural Satellite.
- Stellar evolution preserves a stellar member's stable identity and orbital relationships when its Evolutionary State becomes a Stellar Remnant. Host eligibility is reevaluated rather than silently reassigning dependants.
- The canonical parent describes only the present-day orbit. Formation hosts, migration, capture, and prior parents are separate historical claims.
- Barycentres have no independent radius, composition, Evolutionary State, moons, or rings. Their enclosed mass and position are derived from descendants.
- Construction and deserialization reject unresolved or cross-system references, parent cycles, self-orbits, incompatible host scopes, and accepted parentless members. Ejected or rogue bodies remain outside the current bound-system context.
- The domain permits any nested barycentre as a host. A generator with narrower calibrated coverage returns `Unsupported` rather than weakening the domain model.
