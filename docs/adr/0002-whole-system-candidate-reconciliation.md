# Reconcile immutable candidates at a deterministic whole-system fixpoint

A generated Stellar System is publishable only after one immutable, versioned `Reconciliation Policy` has resolved all candidate conflicts and reached a `Whole-System Fixpoint`. Candidates remain immutable, evidence describes their origin rather than granting physical priority, and every rejection, unsupported case, non-selection, retry, conflict, and unevaluated check remains auditable so scientific occurrence draws are never silently rewritten by plausibility filtering.

## Assembly and authority

Generation follows dependency order: Stellar Orbital Hierarchy, planets, Planetesimal Reservoirs, notable reservoir members, Natural Satellites/Ring Systems/Trojan Populations, Debris Disks and observables, then decorative claims. Domain validators return constraint results; one central reconciler alone applies the policy and decides outcomes. A later phase cannot silently move an accepted prerequisite.

A candidate is an immutable result of a Generating Prescription. Altering its placement creates a separately addressed `Placement Attempt`. Empirical candidates receive no replacement draw after rejection. Proxy and decorative prescriptions may make a positive, versioned, bounded number of attempts only when their prescription defines that process and its conditional distribution. Each attempt has its own `Random Draw Address`; exhaustion never creates a forced replacement.

## Conflicts and outcomes

Individually valid but jointly incompatible candidates form a recorded `Conflict Group`. The policy chooses deterministically by prerequisite dependency, generation phase, and explicitly permitted reconciliation freedom—not by a universal evidence ranking—with stable candidate order as the final tie-breaker. A later candidate loses unless the policy declares the conflict jointly resolvable; only then may an earlier `Provisional Acceptance` be revoked and retained as `Rejected`.

Each failed generated attempt is `Rejected`. After bounded attempts are exhausted, the structure result is `NotSelected` only when the prescription explicitly permits absence; otherwise it remains `Rejected`. `Unsupported` is reserved for missing model coverage. Rejection of an optional structure does not invalidate the rest of the system. A candidate whose required parent or source population is rejected is itself rejected with a reference to that prerequisite and is never deleted or reassigned.

Decorative claims cannot affect physical acceptance. An incompatible decorative attempt may be rejected and retried only inside declared bounds; exhaustion omits the detail, not the physical object.

## Validation and publication

Validators evaluate every relevant constraint they can, rather than stopping at the first failure. The policy classifies constraints as required, advisory, or out of coverage: missing input for a required constraint prevents acceptance; an advisory constraint may remain `NotEvaluated`; a prescription outside model coverage is `Unsupported`. All statuses remain in the `Validation Receipt`.

Changing an input claim transitively invalidates every dependent receipt. Before publication, affected candidates are reevaluated against the final system view. A Stellar System is published only at a deterministic fixpoint with no unresolved conflict groups, current receipts, all required constraints passed, and valid references. Parallel execution cannot change candidate generation, attempt addresses, conflict membership, evaluation order, decisions, or final outcomes for the same seed, Model Realization, prescription versions, and policy version.

## Statistical and audit consequences

An empirical occurrence draw is recorded in its source domain independently of later dynamical acceptance and is never redrawn until it fits. Reports separate generated and accepted empirical candidates by prescription and source domain. Per system and prescription, the audit record retains all `Accepted`, `NotSelected`, `Rejected`, and `Unsupported` outcomes; attempt counts and addresses; complete constraint results; conflict groups; revoked provisional acceptances; required and advisory `NotEvaluated` checks; and generated-versus-accepted empirical counts.
